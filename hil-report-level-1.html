Lumen — Onboarding & First-Edit Validation

A first-look evaluation of Lumen's new-user onboarding and the first AI-powered photo enhancement, where its AI automatically adjusts lighting, color, and composition. Assessed by an independent panel encountering the product for the first time, under real-world conditions.

Lumen is a fictional AI-powered photo enhancement app, used here to demonstrate the format, depth, and evidentiary standard of a Level 1 Real-World Validation Report. Findings, testers, and quotations below are illustrative and do not describe a real product or a real engagement.

Panel
5 testers
Duration
48 hours
Findings
7 issues

Executive Summary

What we were asked to examine

Lumen's team wanted an independent read on the first few minutes of the product experience: account creation, photo library access, and the first AI-enhanced photo. This is the moment that determines whether a new user trusts the app's AI enough to keep using it instead of reverting to manual edits.

Across five independent sessions, evaluators completed the flow without guidance from Lumen's team and without prior knowledge of how the enhancement algorithm was intended to work. We documented seven findings, two of which we rate as high severity because they directly affect whether a first-time user understands and trusts what the AI is doing to their photo.

Methodology

How this examination was conducted

The panel is a small group of independent human testers — not Lumen employees, beta users, or anyone previously familiar with the product. Level 1 engagements can be run with or without SDK or codebase access; this one was assessed as a live product build, with no access to source or internal documentation.

Panel composition

5 testers across iOS and Android, ages 22–54, in the US, UK, and the Philippines, all first-time Lumen users.

Conditions

Real personal devices, real home and mobile network connections, no guidance or hints from Lumen's team during the session.

Scope

Account creation through the first AI-enhanced photo and export. Later features and settings were out of scope for this level.

Findings

Documented observations

Each finding below is corroborated by more than one tester where possible, and includes a specific recommendation rather than a general impression.

01 High

The AI's first enhancement isn't explained

Four of five testers had their first photo brightened, recolored, or recomposed in a way that didn't match what they expected, with no indication of what the AI had changed or why. Two testers described the result as feeling "random" and said they would hesitate to trust the app with a second photo without understanding the first result.

Observed by 4 of 5 testers · Stage: First AI enhancement
Recommendation: Surface a short, specific summary of what changed alongside the result — even "brightened shadows, warmed tone" measurably changes whether a first-time user trusts the feature enough to keep using it.
02 High

Photo library permission request appears before its value is explained

The full photo library permission prompt appears immediately on first launch, before any screen explains why Lumen needs access beyond a single photo. Three testers hesitated or declined on first attempt, then had to restart onboarding to reconnect it once they understood why it was needed.

Observed by 3 of 5 testers · Stage: Account setup
Recommendation: Move the value explanation ahead of the system permission dialog, not after it. A one-screen "why we ask" step ahead of the OS prompt is a well-established pattern for exactly this problem.
03 Medium

Before/after comparison slider resets without warning after saving

Saving an edit snaps the comparison slider back to the "before" position instantly, which two testers found disorienting mid-session — they weren't sure if their edit had actually been saved or reverted.

Observed by 2 of 5 testers · Stage: First edit session
Recommendation: Hold the slider on the "after" position after saving, or show a brief confirmation state, so the save action doesn't read as a reset.
04 Medium

Processing progress indicator doesn't reflect actual remaining time

The "Enhancing..." progress bar reached 90% within two seconds, then stalled there for another eight seconds before completing. One tester tapped away from the screen assuming the app had frozen.

Observed by 2 of 5 testers · Stage: First AI enhancement
Recommendation: Calibrate the progress curve against actual processing time, or replace it with an indeterminate spinner plus a short reassurance message ("finishing touches") during the final stretch.
05 Medium

Device-specific gesture conflict on a Motorola Moto G Power 5G (2024), Android 14

On this device, pinch-to-zoom on the before/after comparison view conflicted with the OS's gesture-navigation back-swipe zone, causing two accidental exits from the app during the first session. This was not observed on the iOS devices or the higher-end Android device (Pixel 8) in the panel.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Device: Motorola Moto G Power 5G (2024), Android 14
Recommendation: Constrain the pinch-to-zoom hitbox away from the screen edge, and test specifically against Android devices with gesture navigation enabled, not just button navigation.
06 Low

Exported photo resolution is lower than the original, with no upfront indication

One tester exported their first edited photo and only noticed afterward, when trying to print it, that the export was compressed well below the original resolution. There was no indication at export time that a lower-resolution file was being produced.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: Export
Recommendation: Show the export resolution before the user taps save, with a clear option to export at full resolution if that isn't already the default.
07 Low

No next-action prompt after the first successful export

One tester who exported their first photo was shown a plain confirmation with nothing else to do or look forward to, which they described as an anticlimactic end to an otherwise positive first session.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: End of first session
Recommendation: Use this moment — it is a natural point to suggest a second photo, show a quick before/after they can share, or invite a second action rather than ending the session flat.

Overall verdict

Lumen's enhancement quality itself is strong, and testers were impressed once they saw a finished result. The risk is concentrated in the first AI enhancement and the photo library permission step — both are early enough in the funnel that a new user's trust is still forming, which is why they are rated high severity despite being fixable with focused, contained changes.

This Level 1 report covers a single flow. A Level 2 or Level 3 engagement would extend this same evidentiary standard across additional flows — such as return-user sessions, settings, and notification behavior — with a larger panel to catch issues a five-person sample won't surface.
