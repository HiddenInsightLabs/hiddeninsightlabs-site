Cadence — Two-Pass Onboarding Validation

A Level 2 evaluation of Cadence's onboarding and first-week habit-building flow, run twice: once to find what was breaking early retention, and again after fixes to verify they actually worked before launch.

Cadence is a fictional habit-building wellness app, used here to demonstrate the format, depth, and evidentiary standard of a Level 2 Real-World Validation Report. Findings, testers, and figures below are illustrative and do not describe a real product or a real engagement.

Executive Summary

What we were asked to examine

Cadence's team suspected their onboarding was losing new users before a habit had a chance to form, but didn't know exactly where or why. They asked us to examine account setup, the first daily check-in, and the reminder-scheduling flow — then, after their engineering team shipped fixes, to verify whether those fixes actually moved the numbers.

Pass 1 surfaced six findings, two of them high severity, with a first-session completion rate of 57%. After two weeks of remediation on Cadence's side, Pass 2 re-ran the identical scope with a fresh set of first-time testers and measured completion at 86%. Four of six findings were fully resolved; one was partially addressed; one remained open at the time of this report.

Methodology

How a two-pass evaluation works

A two-pass engagement exists to answer a different question than a single pass does. Pass 1 tells you what's wrong. Pass 2 tells you whether what you did about it actually worked — with a fresh panel, so the result isn't just familiarity with a fix, but a genuine first-time reaction from someone new.

Pass 1 — Baseline

5 first-time testers across iOS and Android complete onboarding cold, with no guidance. Every drop-off point and point of confusion is documented.

Remediation window

Cadence's team reviews the findings and ships fixes on their own timeline. We do not implement changes ourselves — the verification stays independent.

Pass 2 — Verification

5 new first-time testers, never exposed to the product before, complete the identical scope. Results are compared finding-by-finding against Pass 1.

Pass 1 Findings

What we found before remediation

Six findings, documented against the same evidentiary standard as any Level 1 report — each corroborated across testers where possible, with a specific recommendation attached.

01 Critical

The first daily check-in asks for too much before showing any value

New users are asked to set five separate habit targets, a reminder schedule, and a notification preference before Cadence shows them anything resembling a completed check-in. Three of five testers abandoned mid-setup, most citing that it "felt like too much work" before they'd experienced the product at all.

Observed by 3 of 5 testers · Stage: Account setup
Recommendation: Let a new user complete one full check-in with a single default habit before asking them to configure anything else. Move target-setting and reminder scheduling to after the first success moment.
02 High

No confirmation after the first habit is logged

Testers who completed their first check-in received no acknowledgment beyond the item disappearing from a list. Three testers said they weren't sure the action had registered at all.

Observed by 2 of 5 testers · Stage: First check-in
Recommendation: A brief, specific confirmation — not just a checkmark — reinforces that the action mattered. This is the first rep of the habit loop the whole product depends on.
03 Medium

Reminder time picker defaults to midnight

The reminder scheduling screen defaults to 12:00 AM with no prompt to change it. Two testers set up reminders without noticing the default and only caught it after receiving a midnight notification.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: Reminder setup
Recommendation: Default to a sensible time based on device timezone and time of install, and require an explicit confirm before saving an overnight reminder.
04 Medium

Habit streak resets are not explained

One tester's streak reset after missing a single day, with no in-app explanation of the grace period rules (if any). They described feeling like the app was "punishing" them right after a lapse, which is a risky moment for retention.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: Day 2 return
Recommendation: Whatever the streak logic is, state it plainly at the moment it applies. A missed-day message that explains the rule keeps the moment informative rather than punitive.
05 Low

Onboarding copy refers to "tasks" while the rest of the app says "habits"

A vocabulary mismatch between the setup flow and the main product. No tester was blocked, but it's a small signal of inconsistency in an app whose whole premise is building consistency.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: Account setup
Recommendation: Standardize on "habit" throughout, including onboarding copy and any transactional emails.
06 Low

Notification permission prompt has no fallback path

Testers who declined the OS notification prompt were not offered any in-app way to enable reminders later without going into device settings manually. One tester never returned to complete setup after declining.

Observed by 1 of 5 testers · Stage: Reminder setup
Recommendation: Add a visible in-app path back to notification settings, with a short explanation of what the user is missing without them enabled.
Pass 2 Verification

What changed after remediation

Two weeks after Pass 1, Cadence's team shipped changes addressing most of the findings above. We re-ran the identical scope with ten new first-time testers who had no prior exposure to the product, and measured the result against the same evidentiary standard.

Overall verdict

The two findings most responsible for early drop-off — the overloaded setup flow and the missing check-in confirmation — are both fully resolved, and that shows directly in the completion rate. The one open item (notification fallback) is low severity on its own, but Pass 2 shows it produces the same failure pattern it did in Pass 1, so it's worth closing before wider launch rather than treating it as cosmetic.

This Level 2 report covered three onboarding-adjacent flows with two passes.
