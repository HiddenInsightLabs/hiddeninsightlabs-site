Our Standard

A method built on evidence, not opinion

We borrow our standard of rigor from independent testing and certification bodies — the organizations whose findings are trusted precisely because they have no stake in the outcome.

01

Examination targeted to your concerns

You tell us what worries you most — a flow, a region, a device class, a moment in onboarding. We scope the panel and the examination around that specific risk, not a generic checklist.

02

Evidence over impression

Every finding in a Validation Report is tied to a documented observation — a specific session, a specific user, a specific moment. Conclusions are traceable, not impressionistic.

03

Real conditions, direct measurement

Evaluation happens on real devices, in real network conditions, by people encountering your product for the first time — the same way your actual users will.

Your team has examined this from the inside for months. That proximity is valuable — and it is also exactly why an independent second opinion matters before launch.

Internal teams are the wrong instrument for measuring how a stranger experiences a product, for the same reason a surgeon doesn't evaluate their own outcomes. Not because of any lack of skill or honesty — but because proximity removes the very distance that objective evaluation requires. We provide that distance.

Why This Matters

Your team is too good at knowing your product.

That sounds like a strength. In evaluation, it is a liability.

The more your team understands how something works, the less they can experience it as a stranger would. They skip the confusion. They fill in the gaps. They know which button to press. Your users don't.

This is not a failure of diligence. It is structural — built into how expertise works. The only instrument that can measure how a stranger experiences a product is a stranger.

We work with founders, producers, leads, and CTOs — anyone who knows that a bad launch is not just a bad day. It is a bad quarter, a damaged rating, a window that does not come back.

The teams we work with are talented. They have done their internal testing. They care deeply about quality. And they still want someone outside the building to examine what they have built — because they understand what proximity costs them.

We are not here to replace your QA process. We are the independent layer that turns what proximity makes invisible into documented findings your team can act on before your users find them first.

Common Findings

What we typically find

These patterns appear most consistently across engagements. They require genuine unfamiliarity with the product to detect — which is exactly why internal teams rarely surface them.

01

Confusing onboarding

The first session determines whether a user ever returns. What feels obvious to your team rarely translates to a stranger encountering the product cold.

02

Device-specific failures

Crashes, rendering errors, and performance degradation on real hardware your development environment never tested — particularly mid-range Android devices common in high-growth markets.

03

AI inconsistencies

Adaptive systems that behave confidently but incorrectly for specific user profiles, usage patterns, or edge cases your benchmarks did not include.

04

Regional and network issues

Behavior that degrades meaningfully under real mobile data conditions, in specific markets, or on devices common to your users but absent from your test suite.

05

Trust-breaking UX

Moments where the product does something that makes a user trust it less — not a crash, not an error, but a design decision that reads as careless, misleading, or inconsistent.

06

Hidden friction

Accumulated small hesitations, unclear labels, and unnecessary steps that individually seem minor but collectively cause users to stop and not come back.

Process

How a Validation Report is produced

01

Tell us what you're worried about

We start with what concerns you most about this release — specific flows, risk areas, user types, or platforms. Then we confirm scope, assemble the right panel of human testers, and arrange access to your product. We can work with or without SDK or codebase access depending on your needs.
02

Independent examination

A panel of human testers, unaffiliated with your product, assesses it under realistic conditions — on real devices, on real network connections, without guidance from your team and without prior knowledge of how it was built or what it was supposed to do.
03

Evidence review

Every observation is reviewed, corroborated where possible across testers, and organized into findings with documented support — not summarized impressions.
04

Report and technical walkthrough

You receive a documented Real-World Validation Report, followed by a technical meeting where we walk you through every finding in person and answer questions about what they mean for your release.
